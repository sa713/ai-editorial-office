# Semantic decisions

## Reader-state awareness

**Definition**
Checking whether the reader can understand and safely take the first step.

**When to apply**
Onboarding, participation, change communication, workspace launch.

**Does not mean**
Emotion analysis, motivation strategy, personality targeting.

**Typical mistakes**
Applying it to every text; treating it as persuasion.

**Repair direction**
Tie it to first step, pressure and real obligation level.

## Low-pressure entry

**Definition**
A first step that does not require full participation when full participation is not yet necessary.

**When to apply**
When viewing, reading or trying lightly is factually available.

**Does not mean**
Making mandatory actions sound optional.

**Typical mistakes**
Softening real rules; adding vague friendliness.

**Repair direction**
Name the small honest first action.

## Observation before commitment

**Definition**
Allowing the reader to see the space, examples or options before acting.

**When to apply**
When the reader may need context before joining, creating, replying or submitting.

**Does not mean**
Avoiding action forever.

**Typical mistakes**
No clear next step after observation.

**Repair direction**
Say what to open, what to look at, and when action begins.

## Safe first step

**Definition**
The smallest honest action that helps the reader enter without unnecessary risk.

**When to apply**
When the initial ask may feel too large.

**Does not mean**
Removing accountability.

**Typical mistakes**
Offering a safe step that does not exist.

**Repair direction**
Use a real reversible action: view, read, compare, draft, ask, return later.

## Workspace framing

**Definition**
Describing a tool or process as a working place when that is more accurate and useful.

**When to apply**
Boards, portals, task spaces, shared work areas.

**Does not mean**
Inventing activity or social proof.

**Typical mistakes**
"Everyone is already there" without evidence.

**Repair direction**
Describe what the reader can see or do there now.

## Human operational language

**Definition**
Plain working language that sounds human without becoming promotional.

**When to apply**
Internal communications, instructions, launches.

**Does not mean**
Warmth for its own sake.

**Typical mistakes**
HR-like friendliness, slogans, excessive polish.

**Repair direction**
Use concrete work situations and direct next actions.

## Pressure audit

**Definition**
Review check for unnecessary obligation, urgency, social pressure or early commitment.

**When to apply**
Relevant review tasks only.

**Does not mean**
Judging whether a tone is pleasant.

**Typical mistakes**
Tone policing; subjective preference.

**Repair direction**
Name the pressure source and minimal repair.

## Fake obligation

**Definition**
Text makes optional action sound required.

**When to apply**
When participation or first use is voluntary.

**Does not mean**
Clearly stating real requirements.

**Typical mistakes**
"Need to join" when "can open and look" is true.

**Repair direction**
Restore the actual obligation level.

## Fake momentum

**Definition**
Unsupported suggestion that many people are already using or acting.

**When to apply**
Launches and adoption-sensitive communications.

**Does not mean**
Using sourced usage facts.

**Typical mistakes**
"Everyone already uses it", "active community", "fast-growing".

**Repair direction**
Remove the claim or add evidence.

## Mandatory-process framing

**Definition**
Presenting a working space as a top-down required process.

**When to apply**
Launches of tools, portals, boards, shared spaces.

**Does not mean**
Explaining real process steps when needed.

**Typical mistakes**
Starting with regulations before value or first step.

**Repair direction**
Show what appeared, where it is, and how to enter lightly.

## Pressure-first onboarding

**Definition**
Asking the reader to participate before they understand value or safe entry.

**When to apply**
First-use communications.

**Does not mean**
Clear call to action after context.

**Typical mistakes**
"Create now", "join now", "start using" as the first move.

**Repair direction**
Move to observation or small first step first.

## Overexplaining before entry

**Definition**
Heavy detail before the reader knows why to enter.

**When to apply**
Emails, portal descriptions, instructions.

**Does not mean**
Omitting needed detail.

**Typical mistakes**
Statuses, roles and rules before reader scenario.

**Repair direction**
Put value and first step first; move detail to the right channel.

## Bounded reader-state refinement

**Definition**
Small repair focused on entry friction while preserving facts, structure and governance.

**When to apply**
Review finds pressure or first-step issue, but the artifact is otherwise sound.

**Does not mean**
Full rewrite or tone makeover.

**Typical mistakes**
Changing everything to sound softer.

**Repair direction**
State issue, friction source, minimal repair, do-not-change area, re-review target.

## Operational honesty

**Definition**
The text preserves real facts, obligations, uncertainty and governance while becoming easier to enter.

**When to apply**
Always.

**Does not mean**
Bluntness, legalism or cold tone.

**Typical mistakes**
Hiding constraints to sound welcoming.

**Repair direction**
State constraints clearly and reduce only unnecessary pressure.
