# Usefulness dimensions

## Core idea

Usefulness is not only "what the reader will do next."

Sometimes a text is useful because the reader resists less, trusts more, feels that their state was considered, keeps reading, or accepts a change without feeling treated like a function.

This does not justify long openings, emotional filler, or decorative prose. A usefulness dimension matters only when it changes reader readiness, understanding, trust, acceptance, attention, or action.

## Operational usefulness

Operational usefulness is usefulness of action.

A text is operationally useful when the reader understands:

- what to do;
- when to do it;
- in what order;
- under which conditions;
- how to check that it worked.

This dimension matters most when the text must support execution, compliance, navigation, or next steps.

## Cognitive usefulness

Cognitive usefulness is usefulness of understanding.

A text is cognitively useful when the reader:

- understands a distinction;
- sees a usable model;
- understands a cause;
- can apply the explanation in another situation.

This is not the same as covering the topic fully. The explanation is useful only if it helps the reader interpret, decide, act, trust, or transfer the idea.

## Emotional usefulness

Emotional usefulness is usefulness of working with the reader's state.

It does not mean "write warmer." It means the text may need to:

- acknowledge inconvenience;
- reduce irritation;
- lower anxiety;
- help the reader accept information without a defensive reaction.

For example, in a text about disabling shared door-entry codes, it is not enough to say "use the app." The text may also need to acknowledge that a familiar way of entering the building is changing and that this can be inconvenient.

## Trust usefulness

Trust usefulness is usefulness of justified confidence.

A text is useful for trust when the reader can see:

- why a decision was made;
- what constraints exist;
- what is known and unknown;
- where promises end;
- why the author can be trusted.

Trust usefulness is not the same as reassurance phrases such as "your security is our top priority." Trust comes from visible reasons, limits, evidence, accountability, and proportion.

## Social usefulness

Social usefulness is usefulness of social recognition.

This matters in internal communications, magazines, communities, and team texts. A text can be useful when it helps the reader feel:

- "this is about us";
- "there are real people here";
- "I understand why I am being shown this";
- "I can be part of this."

Social usefulness is not corporate pathos. It does not require claims about talented, inspiring, or outstanding colleagues. It works through specificity, relevance, and a credible sense of shared context.

## Interaction between dimensions

One text can use several usefulness dimensions:

- change communication: operational + emotional + trust;
- educational article: cognitive + trust + emotional;
- internal announcement: social + awareness + operational;
- decision support: cognitive + operational + trust.

One dimension should still be primary. If several dimensions compete for the opening, structure, or review target, the text will sprawl.

## Review implications

Review should ask:

- which usefulness dimension is primary;
- whether an important emotional, trust, or social function was cut by mistake;
- whether "humanity" is hiding the absence of real usefulness;
- whether warmth has become decorative;
- whether warmth weakens clarity, structure, action, or the dominant reader task.

The model should protect useful human material without lowering the standard for usefulness.

## Anti-abuse rules

- Emotional usefulness does not justify essay-mode.
- Warmth is not automatically usefulness.
- Trust usefulness does not justify generic reassurance phrases.
- Social usefulness does not justify corporate pathos.
- Narrative is allowed only when it changes reader readiness, trust, acceptance, attention, or understanding.
- If a lively fragment can be removed without loss to the reader outcome, remove it.
- If a lively fragment reduces resistance or helps the reader accept difficult information, do not automatically treat it as filler.
