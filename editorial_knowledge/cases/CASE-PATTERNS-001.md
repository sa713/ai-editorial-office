# CASE-PATTERNS-001: early editorial system behavior

## Scope

This is a short operational learning note after the first two real editorial cases.

The sample is too small for doctrine changes. The goal is only to record early behavior: what already repeats, where the system is useful, where it may over-correct, and what should remain unchanged for now.

## Cases reviewed

- **CASE-001:** internal announcement for the renewed format of the magazine "Люди УЭК". The task was to help employees notice the new issue, understand what changed, and open it.
- **CASE-002:** internal portal article about one-time passwords. The task was to explain what OTP is, how it works, why it matters, and why an extra authorization action can be useful even when it feels annoying.

## Repeating strengths

The system already seems consistently good at:

- moving discussion from "like / dislike" to reader task;
- detecting intellectual essay-mode;
- limiting context to what changes reader use, interpretation, or action;
- identifying mode overload;
- protecting usefulness as the main review criterion;
- building structure from the reader's task rather than from topic taxonomy.

## Repeating weaknesses

The repeated weakness is that the system cuts excess more confidently than it preserves useful human, emotional, and narrative energy.

In both early cases, this matters. The announcement needs enough warmth to create attention. The OTP article needs to acknowledge the reader's irritation because that irritation is part of the reader state, not decorative color.

This is not yet a reason to rewrite doctrine. It is a behavior to watch.

## Emerging tensions

- **Usefulness vs warmth:** a useful text can still need emotional tact, especially in internal communication.
- **Anti-essay discipline vs useful explanation:** some explanation looks essay-like but is needed for transfer, trust, or acceptance.
- **Compactness vs trust:** shortness can help, but over-compression may remove the reason a reader believes or cares.
- **Review rigor vs lively tone:** review can protect structure while accidentally flattening voice.
- **Mode clarity vs real hybrid tasks:** real tasks often mix awareness, explanation, persuasion, trust, and change communication.

## Dangerous over-corrections

The system may over-correct by:

- drying out an internal announcement until it becomes only a service notice;
- removing reader irritation from the OTP article even though it is an important reader state;
- treating a lively phrase as decorative;
- treating necessary explanation as essay-mode;
- treating a trust-building opening as answer delay.

## What the system handled well

In **CASE-001**, the system would help remove the taste argument and choose a clearer frame: awareness with supporting change communication. It would protect the announcement from becoming a broad reflection on the magazine or a vague corporate celebration.

In **CASE-002**, the system would help catch mode overload and an over-wide scope. It would likely notice that educational explanation, persuasion, awareness, opinion framing, and technical explanation were competing for control of the article.

## What the system did not handle well enough

In **CASE-001**, the system could make the announcement too service-like: clear, short, and useful, but not quite alive enough to make employees want to open the issue.

In **CASE-002**, the system could kill an important part of the article: the explanation of why OTP feels inconvenient and why that inconvenience exists. That reader-state material may be the living reason to read the piece, not a digression.

## What should not be changed yet

After two cases, the system should not:

- add new modes;
- create separate rules for announcements;
- create separate rules for technology articles;
- weaken all anti-essay rules;
- rewrite the review system;
- expand the compact brief;
- conclude that the system urgently needs a new emotional layer.

For now, the right move is continued observation.

## Practical next step

Take a third case of a different type, preferably one of these:

- UX text;
- instruction;
- decision support;
- unpleasant change communication;
- high-stakes internal educational material.

The goal is to test whether the usefulness vs warmth problem repeats, or whether it is mostly a feature of the first two cases.
