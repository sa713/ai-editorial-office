## Editorial Principles

Start from the reader and the task:

- reader first;
- purpose before structure;
- usefulness over completeness;
- structure from task;
- concrete before abstract;
- no essay mode;
- Russian editorial clarity.

Raw, incomplete, ambiguous, or early-stage materials can be valid objects of
analysis. Do not automatically mature them into finished concepts. First
diagnose what is present, unclear, unsupported, or requiring confirmation.

Be disciplined, not defensive. Strong conclusions are allowed when materials
support them. Mark uncertainty only where it changes the conclusion, risk,
recommendation, or next step.

Reader-state awareness is a small sensitivity, not a behavioral layer. It may
clarify a first step or reduce unnecessary pressure, but must not become
persuasion, engagement optimization, emotional editing, or a new workflow.

Honesty rule:

- mandatory stays mandatory;
- optional stays optional;
- unknown stays unknown.

## Operational Rules

Use these as editorial heuristics:

- If the reader needs an answer, recommendation, or decision support, start with
  the answer or decision frame.
- Every section must answer a reader task, not merely cover a topic.
- Do not include context unless it changes interpretation, priority, risk, or
  next action.
- For comparison or choice, structure around decision criteria before options.
- If the next action is known, end with that action or a clear stopping point.
- Start from the reader's current state, not the author's conceptual order.
- For practical content, prefer `condition -> action -> result`.
- Separate facts, interpretation, and recommendation when trust matters.
- In diagnostic analysis, mark confidence only where uncertainty affects the
  analysis.
- Keep editorial diagnosis, author concept diagnosis, and solution design
  separate.
- Remove or demote terms that do not affect reader action.
- Make hidden assumptions explicit when they change validity.
- For scanning, expose hierarchy in headings and first sentences.
- Remove paragraphs that do not advance the reader from question to answer,
  decision, or action.

## Editorial Modes

Modes describe reader interaction, not content formats.

Common modes:

- Decision support: recommendation, criteria, alternatives, risks, next action.
- Operational instruction: prerequisites, steps, checks, exceptions, recovery.
- Orientation: small mental model, key parts, next paths.
- Trust building: claim, evidence, interpretation, safeguards, limits.
- Persuasion or stance: practical claim, reader stakes, objections, ask.
- Awareness: signal, relevance, scope, action or deliberate no-action.
- Change communication: what changed, who is affected, timing, action, support.
- Educational scaffolding: known problem, concept, examples, mistakes,
  application.
- Opinion framing: explicit lens, what it highlights, limits, application.
- Exploration: dimensions, clusters, tensions, narrowing path.
- Diagnosis: observable signal, differentiators, likely causes, tests, next
  action.
- Diagnostic analysis: preserve raw idea state, separate observed, implied,
  inferred, and hypothetical material.

Choose one dominant mode and, when needed, one supporting mode. The supporting
mode must not override the main reader task.

## Compact Editorial Brief

A compact editorial brief is the smallest useful artifact between intake and
writing. It should usually include 5-7 signals that change editorial behavior:

- reader task;
- useful outcome;
- reader state;
- dominant editorial mode;
- structure behavior;
- context limit;
- review target.

Optional signals such as audience, channel, source status, tone, length, legal
or brand risk, and examples become required only when they change usefulness,
risk, or structure.

The brief should change structure before drafting. It is editorial thinking made
visible, not a form to complete.

Anti-bureaucracy rules:

- do not add fields unless they change writing, structure, review, risk, or
  usefulness;
- do not ask for information the system can infer safely;
- do not block drafting because optional signals are missing;
- do not turn modes into a rigid taxonomy;
- do not reward completeness over better editorial decisions.

## Review System

Review verifies usefulness, not polish.

Review priority order:

1. usefulness;
2. reader outcome;
3. mode fit;
4. structural correctness;
5. evidence and reasoning integrity;
6. execution clarity;
7. context discipline;
8. language clarity;
9. stylistic polish.

Review should ask:

- What reader task should the text support?
- What should the reader be able to do, decide, understand, diagnose, trust,
  apply, or explain?
- Does the opening match reader urgency and mode?
- Does structure move from reader need to useful outcome?
- Does each section perform a distinct reader-useful job?
- Is context included only when it changes action, interpretation, risk, or
  trust?
- Are claims, evidence, interpretation, and recommendations separated where
  trust matters?
- Does the ending provide action, decision, stopping point, or usable posture?

Revision is required for reader-task failure, useful-outcome failure, mode
mismatch, answer delay, topic-taxonomy structure, context inflation, mixed
claims/evidence/recommendations, missing execution clarity, or fake completeness.

Do not block acceptance for wording preference when usefulness is intact.

## Failure Patterns

Use failure patterns to name dominant breakdowns and select the smallest repair:

- Fake excitement: replace artificial energy with concrete reader-relevant
  reason to act.
- Institutional opening: move reader-facing change to the first paragraph.
- Answer delay: put answer, change, action, recommendation, or signal first.
- Decorative warmth: keep warmth only when it lowers resistance or clarifies
  relevance.
- Forced inspiration: replace emotion demand with concrete reason for attention.
- Mode blending: choose dominant mode and demote competing jobs.
- Emotional avoidance: name predictable reader discomfort briefly, then return
  to action or support.
- Trust without evidence: show reasoning bridge, limits, and accountability.
- Generic engagement language: state what participation changes and what happens
  next.
- Inherited purpose as hook: replace generic format purpose with the specific
  topic, issue, person, change, or release reason.
