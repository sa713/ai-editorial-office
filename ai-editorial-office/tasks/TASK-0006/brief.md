# Brief

Task ID: `TASK-0006`

Task title: `Task Exchange pilot launch communication`

## Goal

Prepare first launch communication for the Task Exchange pilot:

- corporate email announcement;
- corporate messenger post.

The texts must explain what the Task Exchange is, why it may be useful, how to distinguish a task from an idea, and what the reader should do next.

## Audience

Internal employees, many of whom:

- have not used the system yet;
- know about it only superficially;
- may treat it as another top-down initiative;
- need practical reason to care before reading details.

## Canonical source materials

- `/tasks/TASK-0005/final.md`
- `/tasks/TASK-0004B/final.md`
- `/tasks/TASK-0005/source-draft.md`

These files are canonical for:

- description of the Task Exchange;
- task vs idea distinction;
- onboarding language;
- operational terminology;
- interaction model;
- pilot framing.

## Hard constraints

- Do not invent mechanics, rules, restrictions, promises, or governance logic outside the canonical materials.
- Avoid corporate optimism, fake excitement, campaign tone, HR agitation, and motivational language.
- Avoid claims about efficiency, culture of initiative, new level of interaction, or process improvement as broad slogans.
- Treat this as launch communication, not an instruction, onboarding guide, or celebration.
- Keep the texts operational, calm, useful-first, and adult.

## Required content

Email must include:

- what launched;
- why it is needed;
- when to use it;
- task vs idea distinction;
- basic interaction model;
- where the detailed instruction is;
- what to do next.

Messenger post must:

- be shorter and more scannable than email;
- preserve operational clarity, practical usefulness, launch signal, and non-corporate tone;
- not be a shortened copy of the email.

## Risk mode

`standard`

Rationale: internal launch communication with operational claims about an internal pilot and product use. Source traceability and review are required, but the task has supplied canonical materials and does not require external research.

## Acceptance criteria

- Personal usefulness appears early.
- Launch reason is not buried.
- Explanation does not precede usefulness for too long.
- Text does not sound like another corporate initiative.
- Text does not promise outcomes beyond the pilot materials.
- Email and messenger are structurally aligned but not copies.
- Review gate is completed before finalization.
