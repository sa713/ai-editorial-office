## Checks

### Usefulness

Pass. Both texts give a reader a practical reason to care before detailed process explanation.

Email opens with launch signal and moves immediately to use cases: place a task, respond to a task, or fix an idea before it disappears in correspondence.

Messenger gives the three entry points in the first visible block.

### Launch clarity

Pass. Both versions clearly state that the pilot is launched in АС Taska.

### Onboarding effectiveness

Pass. The email introduces the system without becoming a full instruction. It covers the minimum concepts a first-time reader needs: what it is, when to use it, task vs idea, basic interaction, where to find details, and what to do next.

Messenger is appropriately fast-entry and does not attempt full onboarding.

### Skepticism handling

Pass. The email directly addresses the likely concern that the system is extra reporting or a replacement for team agreements. The tone is calm and operational.

### Signal priority

Pass. Personal usefulness appears early. Launch reason is not buried. Explanation follows usefulness rather than preceding it.

### Reader trust

Pass. The texts avoid fake excitement, motivational framing, and institutional optimism. There is no "we are pleased" opening and no pressure to participate.

### Action discoverability

Pass with non-blocking note. The next step is clear, but final sending requires actual links to replace placeholders.

### Explanation/detail balance

Pass. Email includes enough interaction model to orient readers; detailed fields, statuses, and disputes stay in the instruction. Messenger keeps detail minimal.

### First paragraph earns continued reading

Pass. The first email paragraph is short and direct. The second paragraph quickly establishes why the reader might care.

### Operational realism

Pass. The texts describe concrete actions and agreements rather than abstract benefits.
