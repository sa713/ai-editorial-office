## Findings

No blocking issues.

## Checks

### Usefulness

Pass. The draft defines what openings are for and consistently ties examples to reader-state, context, timing, or action.

### Naturalness

Pass. Most examples sound like plausible internal communication. The language is calm and worklike. It avoids exaggerated warmth.

### Reader Trust

Pass. The guide does not ask readers to feel enthusiasm. It explains why each pattern works and where it should not be used.

### Operational Realism

Pass. The examples fit email and messenger use. Messenger variants are shorter and more scan-friendly.

### Avoidance of Fake Warmth

Pass. The draft explicitly avoids `друзья`, `команда`, playful greetings, and sender-centered enthusiasm.

### Avoidance of Decorative Language

Pass. Examples are functional. The guide does not use literary or campaign-like openings.

### Useful Signal Delay

Pass. The guide repeatedly recommends deleting greetings when they delay the useful signal.

### Real Internal Communication Sound

Pass. The lines are plain enough to be used by actual project owners, not only by professional copywriters.

## Watch Items

- The phrase `Neutral formal` could tempt users to default to weaker openings. The draft counters this by labeling them acceptable, not strong.
- Some examples use product/process placeholders from prior task patterns. This is acceptable because they are illustrative and not presented as factual claims about a current launch.

## Required Changes

None.

## Review Decision

Approved for controlled finalization.
