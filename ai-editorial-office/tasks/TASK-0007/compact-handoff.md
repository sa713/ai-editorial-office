What was produced:

- curated opening patterns for internal email and corporate messenger posts;
- platform split: email vs messenger reading behavior;
- communication type split: news, announcements, calendar/events;
- intensity/formality split: high-clarity operational, neutral formal, softer internal;
- explicit zero-opening cases where the first useful signal should replace greeting;
- acceptable neutral openings with limits;
- bad patterns list with reasons.

Editorial decision:

- opening is useful only when it carries context, timing, relevance, action, or trust;
- greeting-only openings are fallback etiquette, not default structure;
- messenger should usually start with the signal, not a greeting;
- email may use one orienting line only when it helps scanning and interpretation.

Source boundary:

- used user brief and local editorial knowledge only;
- no external research or factual claims added;
- examples are reusable patterns, not mandatory templates.

Before using:

- adapt examples to the actual topic, date, audience, and action;
- do not add fake warmth, sender-centered emotion, playful phrasing, or corporate motivation;
- prefer no opening when a deadline, link, change, or action is already the best first line.

Publication or adoption approval: not granted by the editorial system; remains with the human owner.
