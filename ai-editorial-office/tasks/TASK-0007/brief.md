# Brief

Task ID: `TASK-0007`

Task title: `Communication opening lines`

## Goal

Develop a curated set of opening patterns for internal communications that can replace flat greetings such as:

- `Коллеги, доброе утро`
- `Коллеги, здравствуйте`

The purpose is not creativity for its own sake. The openings must help the reader enter the topic, understand why the message matters, and reach the first useful signal without unnecessary ceremonial delay.

## Audience

Internal communication writers, editors, project owners, and channel owners who prepare:

- corporate email announcements;
- posts in corporate messenger channels.

The end readers are employees who are busy, scan messages quickly, and often decide within the first lines whether a message deserves attention.

## Communication Types

- news;
- announcements;
- event calendar communications.

## Platforms

- email;
- corporate messenger channel.

## Core Editorial Problem

Standard greetings often perform politeness but not orientation. They are acceptable only when the social situation requires a greeting and the useful signal follows immediately. In many internal communications, the first line should instead carry context, relevance, timing, or action.

## Required Output

Create a practical editorial guide with:

- intake conclusions about why openings exist and when they are useless;
- structure-before-writing planning for email and messenger;
- curated opening patterns grouped by platform, communication type, and intensity/formality level;
- good openings;
- acceptable neutral openings;
- cases where no opening is best;
- short notes for each pattern: when to use, why it works, and what reader-state it supports;
- bad patterns list with reasons;
- review and QA artifacts;
- final decision and compact handoff.

## Constraints

Avoid:

- `друзья`;
- `команда`;
- `всем приветики`;
- `рады сообщить`;
- `делимся классной новостью`;
- overly playful tone;
- artificial energy;
- fake warmth;
- corporate sludge;
- marketing copywriting;
- decorative editorial showing off.

Use calm, adult, working language.

## Risk Mode

`low-risk`

Rationale: this is internal editorial guidance with low factual sensitivity, no external publication, no legal or policy claims, and no need for external research. The main risk is editorial quality: fake warmth, inflated tone, and openings that delay usefulness.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Openings are useful, natural, and operationally realistic.
- The guide explains when a greeting helps, when it delays, and when no opening is better.
- Email and messenger differences are explicit.
- Patterns are curated rather than exhaustive.
- Suggestions sound like real internal communication.
- The final guide avoids fake warmth, decorative language, and copywriterly energy.
- Review gate is completed before finalization.
