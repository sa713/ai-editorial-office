## Article Meaning

- Main thought from approved `visual_concept.md`: AI agents are already operating as an enterprise digital workforce, but organizations still lack mature visibility, ownership, runtime controls, traceability, and agent-specific governance for autonomous behavior.

## Key Points

- "Агенты уже работают": AI agents are part of daily enterprise operations, not a future experiment.
- "Много платформ, мало единой картины": fragmented platform adoption makes governance and telemetry harder.
- "Shadow AI появляется рано": unsanctioned agents appear before inventory and oversight mature.
- "Границы нарушаются": agents sometimes exceed intended permissions; scope violations become operational reality.
- "Инциденты тянутся часами": response windows often last hours or longer, increasing exposure.
- "Compliance ≠ стратегия": regulatory frameworks guide governance, but do not replace agent-specific operating controls.
- "Новая задача безопасности": protect behavior, actions, ownership, and audit trails, not only infrastructure access.

## Author Conclusions

- AI agents are becoming part of core workflows and the digital workforce.
- Deployment is moving faster than security and governance maturity.
- Gaps in visibility, ownership, and behavior control are systemic rather than temporary.
- Static-software controls are not enough for autonomous, changing, interconnected systems.
- Organizations should evolve governance and security practices together with agent adoption.

## Visual Hierarchy

- What should be visually central: a moving "digital workforce" of AI agents crossing/pressing against an intended-scope boundary, with nearby guardrails and an audit trail.
- What should be secondary: adoption, shadow agents/inventory, scope violations/incidents, compliance/governance.
- What should be small but still readable: a few numeric anchors: `43%`, `21%`, `53%`, `47%`, `13%`.

## Drawing Elements

- Simple drawings, symbols, or metaphors:
  - Small AI-agent nodes with legs/tools: the autonomous digital workforce.
  - Dashed perimeter/guardrail: intended scope.
  - Foggy map with missing pins: incomplete inventory.
  - Question-mark owner tags: unclear accountability.
  - Footprints or dotted action trail: traceability/audit evidence.
  - Alert triangle and clock: incidents and response time.
  - Control rail with four bolts: registry, ownership, runtime policy, logs.
  - Compliance stamp/checklist: useful but incomplete governance starting point.
- What each drawing element means: preserve the source argument that security must govern autonomous actions in context, not merely static systems.

## Do Not Show

- What must not be drawn: photorealistic robots, evil AI villains, a polished corporate dashboard, product UI, vendor logo, glossy shield-only security metaphor.
- What must not be written: claims that all agents are malicious, that compliance is worthless, or that one tool fixes the problem.
- Which distortions or invented theses to avoid: do not invent recommendations beyond the article's control categories; do not add threat actors or breach scenarios not in the source.
