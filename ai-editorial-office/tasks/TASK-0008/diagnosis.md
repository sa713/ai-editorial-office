# Diagnosis

## short answer

The communication fails because it sells an atmosphere and intellectual ambition before it defines an operating model.

The idea itself is not weak. A recurring internal cyber-security discussion club could be useful: it can broaden perspective, surface expertise, create a low-friction speaking format, and make strategic cyber topics discussable outside day-to-day delivery.

But the materials do not reliably answer the operational questions a stakeholder needs:

- What exactly is being launched?
- Is this a lunch, a discussion club, a speaker series, part of "Выравнивание", or a culture initiative?
- Who owns it?
- Who is expected to do what next?
- Who is the core audience?
- What practical value will participants get?
- What decision is leadership being asked to make?
- What execution system makes this repeatable?

The communication is therefore not just "too long". It is under-architected.

## what the author likely tried to communicate

The author appears to be proposing a recurring internal discussion format for УЭК where employees discuss cyber-security topics in a broader strategic, technological, cultural, and geopolitical context.

The intended activity likely has these components:

- one speaker introduces a topic briefly;
- participants discuss it;
- topics are connected to cyber security and to УЭК's work;
- the activity builds shared awareness and professional culture;
- employees can become speakers and show expertise;
- sessions should feel more intellectually attractive than a routine status meeting.

The author also seems to want the format to have status. Phrases like "культурный код", "престижное интеллектуальное событие", "лидеры мысли", and "каждый уважающий себя эксперт" show a wish to make the activity aspirational.

## where understanding likely broke

The deck gives a concept, but not a decision package.

It says what the event could feel like and what benefits it could produce, but leaves unresolved:

- final format;
- cadence;
- owner;
- launch sequence;
- topic selection;
- speaker pipeline;
- participant promise;
- how success will be judged.

The email tries to resolve the meeting aftermath, but it expands instead of clarifying. It introduces a new frame, "гибрид-синтез", and adds criteria, framing, cadence, event status, and a sample teaser. This makes the reader infer the actual decision from a dense explanation.

The likely result: leadership hears an interesting but still unready idea; executors receive enthusiasm and possible ingredients but not a work order; participants are not yet given a crisp reason to attend.

## main failure

The communication is idea-forward instead of work-forward.

It talks about the value, culture, theme space, intellectual posture, and possible tone of the activity before it establishes the service model:

```text
Кибер-ланчи are a monthly 40-minute internal discussion session where one УЭК speaker introduces a cyber-security topic in 15 minutes and then moderates a 20-25 minute discussion. The activity is owned by X, promoted through Y, uses Z topic criteria, and is meant to give employees practical context, shared vocabulary, and a visible channel for expert exchange.
```

That sentence is reconstructable from the materials, but the materials never give it in one place.

## buried main idea

The core idea is distributed across slides and email:

- slide 1 gives the name and atmosphere;
- slide 2 gives rough format and broad topic territory;
- slide 3 gives audience and mechanics, but with unresolved questions;
- slide 4 and 5 give benefits;
- slide 7 gives possible participation modes;
- the email changes cadence and positioning, moving from "2 раза в месяц" to "раз в месяц, по пятницам" and from "ланч-сессии" to "Дискуссионный Клуб";
- the long teaser demonstrates one possible event but overwhelms the operational point.

Because the main idea is not answer-first, each reader must assemble it manually.

## missing operational model

The materials do not define:

- owner: who runs the series;
- sponsor: who approves and protects the time;
- calendar mechanics: exact cadence, day, room, invite, remote option;
- topic intake: how topics are proposed, selected, and approved;
- speaker preparation: who helps speakers shape topics and teasers;
- moderation: what the moderator must do before, during, and after;
- artifacts: whether sessions produce notes, recordings, links, or follow-up posts;
- participation loop: how feedback changes future sessions;
- success criteria: attendance, repeat speakers, topic usefulness, feedback, or cultural effect.

The source says "как можно включиться" but does not define the operating system that makes involvement possible.

## overloaded explanation

The author repeatedly explains why the idea is meaningful, but not enough how it works.

Examples:

- "формируем единое видение КБ используя разные аналитически-тематические линзы" is intellectually plausible but abstract;
- "культурный код" and "престижное интеллектуальное событие" express aspiration but do not tell executors what to build;
- the splinternet teaser is vivid, but in a post-meeting email it competes with the actual decision and next steps.

The writing performs the author's intelligence. It shows range, context, and conceptual ambition. But it does not transfer a compact operating picture to the reader.

## audience mismatch

The same material tries to serve three different audiences:

- leadership needs decision clarity: purpose, resource cost, risk, expected effect, sponsor ask;
- executors need an operating model: owner, cadence, process, next steps, copy needed, deadlines;
- participants need a simple promise: what happens, why it is worth attending, how to join.

The deck mixes all three layers. The email mainly speaks to executors, but still uses leadership- and participant-facing rhetoric. No audience receives a fully appropriate layer.

## relationship between deck and email

The email does not close the gaps left by the deck. It inherits them and adds more interpretive load.

The deck is a concept pitch with unresolved operational questions. The email should have converted discussion into decisions:

- agreed;
- not agreed;
- proposed decision;
- required owner decisions;
- next actions.

Instead, it presents a synthesis in prose. It adds useful choices, especially the 15+25 minute format and clearer topic criteria, but it does not distinguish decision from proposal, or action from example.

## strongest side of the idea

The strongest version of the idea is not "lunches". It is a lightweight internal forum for strategic cyber-security sensemaking.

Potential practical value:

- helps employees connect cyber-security work to broader technological, geopolitical, and human contexts;
- creates a repeatable channel for experts to test and share perspectives;
- develops speaking and moderation skills inside УЭК;
- gives teams reusable arguments, examples, and vocabulary;
- may reveal emerging concerns and questions earlier than formal meetings.

This value is real only if the format stays operationally small and clear. If it becomes an abstract prestige project, it will lose participants.

## likely communication strategy needed

The strategy should separate layers:

1. Leadership layer: "what decision do we need and why is it worth the time?"
2. Operating layer: "how exactly will the activity run?"
3. Executor layer: "what do we need to do this week?"
4. Participant layer: "why attend and what will happen in the room?"
5. Support layer: "how to propose a topic, become a speaker, and prepare a teaser."

The current communication tries to compress all five into a deck and a long email. That is the core structural failure.
