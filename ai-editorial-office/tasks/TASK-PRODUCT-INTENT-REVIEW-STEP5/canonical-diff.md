# Canonical Diff — Step 5

## baseline

Before Step 5, the canonical owner required a hypothesis, context, minimum
intervention, observable signal, continue/reconsider conditions, and inference
limits, and rejected fabricated metrics and general proof claims.

## added semantics

| Area | Step 4 baseline | Step 5 canonical behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | Validate when a material hypothesis remains. | Start from the main gap and decide `minimum_test`, `not_needed`, or `insufficient`. |
| Criticality | Bounded hypothesis. | One critical hypothesis plus why the next costly or hard-to-reverse decision depends on it. |
| Uncertainty | Generic material hypothesis. | Problem, demand, mechanism, behavior, usability, feasibility, or viability. |
| Method | Minimum ethical/practical validation. | Fitted bounded method derived from uncertainty, risk, cost, reversibility, evidence, context, and constraints. |
| Minimality | Minimum intervention or exposure. | Explicitly cheaper than full implementation, stoppable, reversible, and free of unrelated features/production finishing. |
| Signal | Observable action, signal, state, or contradiction. | Preferred real-signal kinds plus rejection of weak attitudes as proof. |
| Decision rule | Continue and reconsider. | Observable qualitative conditions allowed; numeric threshold requires a basis. |
| Boundary | What the observation does not prove. | Specific inference examples plus mandatory next owner decision. |
| Sequence | No full-research default. | Only the nearest decision-relevant check; full sequential programs require separate authority. |
| AI | General evidence limits. | Data quality, variability, model limits, human control, privacy, permitted use, and work-effect checks. |
| Reader result | Practical minimum validation. | What to test, how to test, what to observe, how to decide, and what it cannot prove. |
