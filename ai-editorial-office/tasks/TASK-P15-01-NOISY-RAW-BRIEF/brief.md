# Brief

## raw request summary

User asks to prepare a post about a new employee interests dashboard. The raw
request includes dissatisfaction that department news are not being read and
that previous texts feel boring and corporate.

## user goal

- confirmed: create a post about the new employee interests dashboard;
- inferred: make the post more readable/engaging than previous boring,
  corporate-feeling texts;
- unknown: what concrete action the post should drive.

## audience / reader

- confirmed: unknown;
- inferred: likely readers are connected to department news or employees, but
  the exact audience is not stated;
- unknown: whether the audience is all employees, one department, managers, or
  another group.

## expected artifact

- confirmed: post;
- inferred: internal communication task;
- unknown: publication channel, length, format, call to action, and required
  variants.

## source status

- supplied sources: none;
- source status: `mentioned but not provided`;
- source boundary: the dashboard is mentioned by the user, but no details,
  links, screenshots, product description, audience data, or launch context are
  available.

## constraints

- Do not write the post during intake.
- Do not invent publication channel.
- Do not invent exact audience.
- Do not turn user frustration into a separate content requirement.
- Avoid making the future post boring or overly corporate, because the user
  explicitly says previous texts had that problem.

## explicit requirements

- Write a post.
- Topic: new employee interests dashboard.
- The dashboard should be presented as useful, but the exact benefits are not
  supplied.
- Avoid a boring or corporate-sounding result.

## assumptions

- The task is a writing task, not research, review, visual, or UX-writing.
- The post is likely for internal communication, because the request mentions
  department news and employee interests.
- Dashboard details must be supplied or constrained before writing starts.

## open questions

- Who exactly should read the post?
- Where will the post be published?
- What is the dashboard's purpose and what can employees do with it?
- Which benefits are confirmed and may be claimed?
- Is there a link, screenshot, release note, source description, or product
  owner input for the dashboard?
- What should the reader do after reading: open the dashboard, leave interests,
  share feedback, or simply learn that it exists?

## acceptance criteria

- The task definition separates task signal, background context, noise,
  confirmed facts, inferred context, unknowns, assumptions, questions, source
  status, and acceptance criteria.
- The normalized brief does not invent audience, channel, dashboard functions,
  benefits, metrics, source materials, or final copy.
- The future post, if production proceeds after clarification, should be about
  the new employee interests dashboard and should avoid boring/corporate tone.

## suggested task type / pipeline

- Suggested task type: social/internal communications writing task.
- Suggested pipeline: social or compact writing pipeline after Chief Editor
  confirms route and preflight strategy.
- Missing data strategy: `ask` or `constrain` before writing, because source
  status, audience, channel, and success criterion are incomplete.

## risks

- Writing now would require inventing dashboard details.
- Treating "nobody reads department news" as proof about the target audience or
  channel would overread the request.
- Treating frustration as a requirement could distort the brief.
- Claiming the dashboard is useful without details would be unsupported beyond
  the user's vague wording "вроде полезный".
