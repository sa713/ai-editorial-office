# Care as a Legible Internal Service

## Editorial Decision

The brainstorm should not be turned into an immediate PR campaign. The stronger
route is a service-positioning program: first make Care understandable,
reachable, useful, and internally consistent; then increase visibility through
evidence of work.

This matters because the source does not describe a simple awareness problem.
It points to several linked issues: people may not understand Care's value,
interaction model, responsibilities, outputs, and internal way of working. If
Care starts by advertising itself before clarifying those things, the campaign
can make the weak spots more visible instead of solving them.

## Source Boundary

The brainstorm is enough to define a working hypothesis. It is not enough to
state that Care's reputation is objectively low across UEC.

Use the source this way:

- as a signal that Care needs clearer positioning;
- as a map of perceived problem zones;
- as a starting point for validation with UEC audiences.

Do not use it as proof that all UEC employees think the same way.

## Reframed Problem

The working problem is not "Care needs more PR." It is:

> Care needs to become a legible internal service: people should understand what
> Care owns, when to come to Care, what help they can expect, who to contact,
> and what visible results Care already produces.

This reframing keeps the image question, but moves it from cosmetic visibility
to service clarity.

## Strategic Principles

1. Clarify before amplifying. A portfolio, status meeting, or "Made by Care"
   label works better when Care has a clear service promise.
2. Validate before promising. The brainstorm names likely needs, but UEC
   employee needs should be checked before Care commits to solutions.
3. Show useful evidence. Visibility should come from concrete artifacts:
   service map, portfolio, status, clear entry points, and better publication
   quality.
4. Treat Care communications as a product. Posts, mailings, announcements, and
   visuals need a production process, owners, quality bar, and review.
5. Fix the internal rhythm where it affects external clarity. Moderated syncs,
   activity boards, and role clarity are not internal housekeeping only; they
   shape how reliably Care can serve others.

## Workstreams

### 1. Service Map

Goal: explain what Care does and how employees interact with it.

Outputs:

- Care responsibility map;
- list of request types Care accepts;
- simple "come to us when..." guide;
- contact/entry-point map by question type;
- internal role map for Care members.

Why first: without this, any PR activity risks sending people to an unclear
service.

### 2. UEC Needs Discovery

Goal: replace assumptions with a lightweight evidence base.

Actions:

- interview or survey several audience segments: leadership, leads, experts,
  product teams, and internal functions;
- ask what they think Care does now;
- ask when they would come to Care;
- ask what currently makes interaction with Care hard;
- ask what Care could do that would make their work easier.

Output: short needs map with confirmed needs, misunderstood areas, and
questions that remain open.

### 3. Visibility System

Goal: make Care work visible without turning every artifact into self-promotion.

Actions:

- create a Care portfolio with selected examples and the problem each example
  solved;
- define where "Made by Care" is appropriate and where it would be distracting;
- return half-yearly Care status sessions if leadership and audience need them;
- create a repeatable format for "what Care shipped / what changed / how to ask
  for similar help."

Output: portfolio plus status format.

### 4. Publication Quality Process

Goal: stop Care materials from looking accidental or "for the checkbox."

Actions:

- define a small publication workflow for mailings, posts, announcements, news,
  posters, and similar materials;
- create a tone and quality checklist;
- decide who writes, who reviews, and who approves;
- keep reusable artifacts: editorial policy, tone of voice, brand basics, and
  examples of good materials.

Output: publication process and minimum quality standard.

### 5. Internal Operating Rhythm

Goal: reduce internal friction that weakens external service.

Actions:

- moderate Care syncs with timeboxes and explicit agenda ownership;
- keep a Kanban board for visible activities and ownership;
- separate broad team topics from two- or three-person problem solving;
- hold an internal discussion on Care's group-level functions and boundaries;
- identify where mutual backup is weak and document the minimum coverage model.

Output: operating rhythm that makes Care's work easier to understand and
continue.

## 90-Day Route

### Days 1-30: Align Care Internally

Decisions:

- agree that the goal is service clarity plus visibility, not PR alone;
- choose one owner for the initiative;
- approve the first draft of the Care service map;
- define which audience segments will validate it first.

Artifacts:

- draft service map;
- draft request-type list;
- draft role/contact map;
- publication quality checklist v1;
- Care activity board.

### Days 31-60: Validate With UEC

Decisions:

- choose interview or survey format;
- decide which feedback changes the service map;
- identify two or three visibility pilots.

Artifacts:

- needs map;
- revised service map;
- first portfolio skeleton;
- "Made by Care" usage rule;
- status-session format.

### Days 61-90: Pilot And Package

Decisions:

- decide what becomes official;
- decide whether to run a Care status session;
- decide which communication channels will carry Care visibility.

Artifacts:

- Care portfolio v1;
- official or pilot service map;
- publication workflow;
- first status narrative;
- list of follow-up improvements.

## First Practical Move

Start with a 90-minute Care internal workshop:

1. agree on what Care owns;
2. list the top request types Care wants to receive;
3. list what Care should not own;
4. choose three UEC audience segments for validation;
5. assign owners for the service map, needs discovery, publication process, and
   portfolio.

This workshop creates the foundation for every visible action that follows.

## Decisions Needed Before Rollout

- Who owns the initiative inside Care?
- Which parts of the plan can be discussed with UEC employees before leadership
  approval?
- Which artifacts are internal drafts and which can become official?
- What evidence is needed before saying Care's positioning has improved?

## Success Criteria

The work is succeeding if, after the first cycle:

- Care can explain its function in one page;
- employees know where to bring common questions;
- Care has a small portfolio of visible, attributable work;
- Care materials pass a basic quality check before publication;
- internal Care activities have owners and are visible enough to reduce
  repeated status questions;
- the team has evidence from UEC employees, not only internal assumptions.
