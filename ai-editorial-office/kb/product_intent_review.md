# Product Intent Review

This file is the sole canonical owner of Product Intent Review purpose, scope,
activation and non-activation semantics, depth modes, product-intent model,
checks, incomplete-data behavior, main-gap logic, alternative classes, minimum
hypothesis validation, adaptive output semantics, authority boundaries, role
cooperation, and failure modes.

Product Intent Review is a conditionally activated, evidence-bounded analytical
lens inside the Professional Analysis family. It is not a new role, pipeline,
lifecycle stage, review gate, task status, operational review outcome, workflow,
or mandatory task-local artifact.

## Operational Status

Product Intent Review is implemented, end-to-end evaluated, and available for
conditional activation in the current AI Editorial Office operating model.
This status applies only to this specialized lens. Professional Analysis
remains an open release candidate, and use of its current parent contract here
is backward-compatible rather than acceptance of the whole capability family.

The capability is loaded only when the Chief Editor selects `limited` or
`full`. Obvious `not_needed` work remains on the compact path and does not
require a Product Intent block, owner load, or heavy task state.

## Purpose

Product Intent Review is used before deep editorial work when the usefulness of
the requested artifact materially depends on whether a proposed product,
service, intervention, experience, or change mechanism makes sense.

It helps the existing task owners:

- reconstruct the available product intent without inventing it;
- test whether the proposed intervention has plausible value and fit;
- examine whether the proposed mechanism could produce the required change;
- expose viability limits before extensive production;
- identify one main product gap that could change the next decision;
- name bounded alternative classes without choosing for the product owner;
- define the minimum useful validation of a material hypothesis;
- state what editorial production may safely assume, preserve, question, or
  postpone.

The lens produces evidence-bounded decision support. It does not confirm a
market, user need, causal effect, product success, or launch decision unless the
task evidence actually supports that conclusion.

## Applicability

Product Intent Review can apply to product or intervention concepts including:

- educational activities, courses, exercises, and learning interventions;
- internal services and employee experiences;
- communication mechanisms and behavior-change communications;
- events and facilitated experiences;
- organizational change programs;
- new or changed processes;
- tools and operational aids;
- user scenarios and service journeys;
- digital or non-digital product concepts;
- other interventions whose intended value depends on a change in action,
  experience, understanding, capability, or operating result.

Commercial intent, a digital interface, revenue, and a conventional software
product are not prerequisites. The relevant question is whether the requested
editorial work is materially inseparable from an unapproved product or
intervention intent.

## Parent Capability And Governance Boundary

Product Intent Review is a specialized lens within the current Professional
Analysis family:

- Professional Analysis owns the general analytical product shape, synthesis,
  implications, options, recommendations, uncertainty, and next action;
- Product Intent Review owns only the narrow product-intent semantics defined
  in this file;
- use of this relationship does not accept, release, broaden, or finalize the
  Professional Analysis release candidate;
- Product Intent Review does not authorize a future project stage or any
  production behavior by itself.

The historical Problem Hypothesis proposal remains a separate, unaccepted
proposal. Product Intent Review's `problem` element does not adopt that
proposal's workflow, artifact, or routing contract. Incidental existing
references to Problem Hypothesis do not make it a canonical Product Intent
Review dependency.

## Non-Goals

Product Intent Review does not:

- create a role, pipeline, lifecycle stage, review gate, task status, review
  outcome, or mandatory task artifact;
- replace a product owner or take a launch, investment, scope, pilot, or stop
  decision for that owner;
- claim market confirmation, audience demand, product-market fit, or user need
  without sufficient evidence;
- perform full user research, market research, service design, or product
  discovery by default;
- replace domain expertise or make domain conclusions without source-backed
  domain evidence;
- guarantee effectiveness, adoption, learning, behavior change, or business
  outcome;
- become a universal strategy audit, universal brief, or general consulting
  framework;
- create its own evidence taxonomy, confidence scale, or universal scoring
  model;
- duplicate Analytical Reasoning, Audience and Outcome Alignment, Editorial
  Planning, Architecture Review, Editorial Challenge, or Review Agent;
- turn every editorial deliverable into a product-design assignment;
- force a complete seven-field record when the evidence cannot support it;
- make editorial production responsible for resolving a product decision that
  belongs to the product owner.

## Known Limitations

- Product Intent Review cannot confirm a market, demand, need, or product value
  without suitable external or task-supplied evidence.
- Product judgment quality is bounded by the completeness, relevance,
  freshness, and credibility of the available evidence.
- Complex, specialized, high-risk, legal, ethical, safety, financial,
  architecture, engineering, or domain decisions may require their existing
  expert or human owners.
- Minimum Product Validation reduces one decision-relevant uncertainty; it does
  not prove full effectiveness.
- One pilot, prototype, exercise, interview, or observed case does not establish
  scalability, persistence, transfer, causal effect, or sustainable operation.
- The capability advises but does not decide product direction, investment,
  scope, intervention class, pilot, launch, or stop for the product owner.
- `limited` and `full` require professional Chief Editor judgment; they are not
  selected automatically by keywords, scores, or thresholds.
- The evaluation suite covers bounded representative classes and adversarial
  cases, not every domain, product, risk, or execution context.
- Professional Analysis remains an open release candidate.
- Product Intent Review is not a full-cycle product studio, market-research
  function, product-management role, or substitute for real-world ownership.

## Activation Contract

Activation is evidence-led and multi-signal. A keyword such as “product,”
“course,” “service,” “campaign,” “event,” “tool,” “process,” “UX,” or
“strategy” is never sufficient by itself.

Product Intent Review is materially indicated when at least two mutually
reinforcing signals are present, or when one high-consequence signal makes
product uncertainty inseparable from editorial usefulness:

- the task concerns a new, materially changed, or not-yet-approved product,
  service, intervention, process, experience, or operating mechanism;
- the requested work expects a change in user, learner, employee, stakeholder,
  or operator behavior or experience;
- the proposed solution class is not approved and editorial production could
  prematurely lock it in;
- the decision materially concerns viability, pilotability, no-build,
  reduce-scope, change-class, or stop;
- the artifact's usefulness depends on assumptions about product value,
  intervention fit, causal mechanism, or adoption;
- the deliverable and the proposed product/intervention are materially
  inseparable, so polishing the deliverable could conceal a weak product intent;
- evidence conflict or a critical unknown could change whether production
  should continue, narrow, test, change class, or stop.

Activation requires a task-local reason that identifies the supporting signals
and the consequence of skipping the lens. The activation reason is advisory
input to the existing Chief Editor routing decision; this specification does
not implement routing.

## Non-Activation Contract

Product Intent Review is normally not needed when:

- the work is a simple edit, proofread, translation, shortening, formatting, or
  tone adjustment with no material product decision;
- the task is local UI text inside an approved product concept and approved
  interaction model;
- the intervention, solution class, audience, mechanism, and success boundary
  are already approved, evidenced, and outside the requested scope;
- product analysis cannot materially change the usefulness, safety, scope, or
  next decision for the requested artifact;
- the task only asks for faithful source conversion or summarization and does
  not authorize challenging the underlying intervention;
- a specialized owner such as Architecture Review or Engineering Review fully
  covers the actual decision and no broader product-intent question remains.

If activation evidence is weak, record `not_needed` rather than inventing a
product problem. If one narrow product assumption affects the artifact, use
`limited` rather than expanding the whole task.

## Depth Modes

Product Intent Review has three task-local depth values:

| Value | Use | Minimum behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `not_needed` | Activation signals are absent or immaterial to editorial usefulness. | Record only the task-local non-activation reason when traceability is useful. |
| `limited` | One or a few product-intent assumptions materially constrain the artifact, but a full reconstruction would be disproportionate or unsupported. | Examine only the affected elements/checks, state evidence limits, name the main gap if one is decision-relevant, and record consequences for production. |
| `full` | Several product-intent elements or checks are materially uncertain, the solution class is unapproved, or the next decision could change class. | Use the complete relevant model, all four checks, one main-gap decision, bounded alternatives, and minimum validation when a material hypothesis remains. |

These values are not task statuses, pipeline stages, review levels, lifecycle
stages, review verdicts, product findings, or launch decisions. They do not
create a separate artifact requirement. Use the smallest existing task artifact
that keeps the analysis reviewable and restartable.

## Seven-Element Product Intent Model

Reconstruct only what the evidence can support. The seven elements are a
reasoning model, not a form that must always be fully populated.

| Element | Core question | Minimum useful content | Absence or weakness signals | Inference boundary | Insufficient-data behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Who is expected to receive value or change, and in what material context? | A bounded audience or actor group plus the relevant use, work, learning, or decision context. | “Everyone,” only demographics, conflicting audiences, no context, or an audience inferred only from channel. | Do not invent needs, motivation, behavior, access, capability, demand, or representativeness. | Preserve the known context, label the audience as a hypothesis or unknown, and state which decision cannot be audience-specific. |
| Problem | What evidence-bounded difficulty, unmet need, opportunity, risk, or job makes intervention worth considering? | The observed or credibly reported condition, who experiences it, and why it matters to the decision. | Solution-first wording, vague dissatisfaction, no evidence, no consequence, or a problem defined only as absence of the proposed solution. | Do not treat stakeholder preference, a request, or one anecdote as proof of a general need. | State the proposed problem as a hypothesis, identify the missing evidence, and avoid product-value claims. |
| Required change | What observable change in action, experience, understanding, capability, decision, or operating condition is needed? | A directional before/after difference that does not presume the solution. | Output-only goal, abstract “engagement,” activity counts, or a change that cannot be distinguished from delivery. | Do not invent magnitude, timeline, motivation, causality, or universal success thresholds. | Keep the intended direction provisional and state what observable distinction is unavailable. |
| Proposed product or intervention | What is being proposed, at what scope, and what class of intervention is it? | A bounded concept, solution class, material components, exclusions, and current maturity or approval state. | Feature list without intent, shifting scope, artifact described as the whole product, or unapproved concept treated as fixed. | Do not add features, channels, service promises, operating capacity, or owner commitments. | Name only the known class or components; keep the rest unknown and avoid feasibility conclusions. |
| Mechanism | Through what plausible causal or behavioral chain could the intervention contribute to the required change? | A bounded “because” chain linking exposure or use to an intermediate action or condition and then to the intended change. | Magical causality, content exposure treated as outcome, missing intermediate action, or mechanism borrowed from an unrelated context. | Do not claim causality, transfer, adoption, or persistence beyond the available evidence. | State a mechanism hypothesis, its critical assumption, and the minimum signal that could challenge it. |
| UX | What must a person notice, understand, trust, choose, do, and sustain for the mechanism to operate? | The essential journey or interaction condition, barriers, and owner-approved constraints relevant to adoption or completion. | Idealized user, no friction, no entry or completion condition, coercive assumption, or interface treated as the whole experience. | Do not invent motivation, accessibility, consent, trust, operational support, or usability evidence. | Identify the unknown experience condition and constrain editorial claims or production that depend on it. |
| Observable result | What observable action, signal, state, or decision would show whether the intent deserves continuation or reconsideration? | A result close enough to the mechanism to observe and bounded enough to inform the next decision. | Vanity metrics, output volume, universal KPI, distant outcome without attribution, or an unobservable claim. | Do not invent baselines, targets, sample sizes, significance, causal attribution, or guaranteed effect. | Name the type of signal if possible, state missing measurement context, and limit the decision it could support. |

The model must preserve the difference between:

- confirmed task evidence;
- a bounded hypothesis or inference;
- an unknown that the current sources do not resolve.

Evidence classes, confidence labels, assumptions, unknowns, and validation
requirements remain owned by `/kb/editorial_evidence_framework.md`.

## Four Product Checks

Use all four checks in `full` mode and only the material subset in `limited`
mode.

### Value

Ask:

- Is there credible evidence of a meaningful problem, need, opportunity, or
  risk for a bounded audience or context?
- Would the required change matter enough to justify any intervention now?
- Is the proposed value distinct from producing the requested artifact?
- What is confirmed, hypothesized, unknown, or contradicted?
- What would make no intervention or a smaller intervention preferable?

### Fit

Ask:

- Does the intervention class fit the audience, context, problem, constraints,
  timing, access, and decision owner?
- Is the requested deliverable the right product component, or is it being used
  as a proxy for a missing service, process, interface, or decision?
- What assumptions about use, motivation, capability, trust, or adoption are
  unsupported?
- Would a different intervention class fit the context better?

### Mechanism

Ask:

- Is there a plausible evidence-bounded chain from intervention to required
  change?
- Which user or operator action and intermediate condition must occur?
- Which assumption is most critical to that chain?
- Is exposure, publication, attendance, completion, or content consumption
  being mistaken for the intended outcome?
- What observation could challenge the mechanism before full production?

### Viability

Ask:

- Can the intervention be delivered, used, supported, and sustained inside the
  known constraints?
- Are essential owner, operational, legal, ethical, accessibility, technical,
  timing, or evidence dependencies unknown?
- Is the concept small enough to test without pretending to validate the whole
  product?
- Should the next decision be continue, reduce, test, change class, research
  first, or stop?

Viability is not a substitute for Architecture Review, Engineering Review,
legal review, safety review, finance, operations, or domain expertise. Route
specialized questions to their existing owners.

## Incomplete And Conflicting Data

When data is incomplete:

1. reconstruct only the elements supported by available evidence;
2. separate confirmed evidence, bounded hypotheses, and unknowns;
3. do not invent audience, market, need, motivation, behavior, effect,
   operating capacity, constraints, baselines, or metrics;
4. do not force all seven elements to appear;
5. perform a `limited` review if a bounded decision-relevant check is still
   possible;
6. state which product or editorial decisions cannot currently be made;
7. identify the smallest evidence or owner decision that would reduce the
   material uncertainty;
8. preserve contradictions instead of averaging or resolving them without
   basis;
9. calibrate claims and confidence through the Editorial Evidence Framework.

Insufficient evidence is not automatically a `blocked` operational verdict.
The existing Review Agent and lifecycle own that verdict. Product Intent Review
may instead conclude that the product finding is provisional, that production
must be constrained, or that a product-owner decision is not yet supportable.

## One Main Product Gap

Prioritize one main product gap when a gap is material. It is the single
uncertainty, contradiction, mismatch, or unsupported assumption most capable of
changing the next decision class:

- continue;
- reduce scope;
- test;
- change intervention class;
- research before production;
- stop.

Select it by:

1. decision consequence;
2. proximity to the value or causal mechanism;
3. evidence weakness or contradiction;
4. cost of locking the assumption into editorial production;
5. whether a smaller check could materially reduce uncertainty.

Do not replace prioritization with a flat list of many issues. Secondary issues
may be retained only when they affect the interpretation of the main gap,
specialized risk routing, or a non-relaxable constraint.

## Alternative Classes

When the main gap weakens the proposed intervention, name bounded alternative
classes rather than silently redesigning the product. Relevant classes may
include:

- a smaller intervention;
- a different intervention class;
- a short pilot;
- research before production;
- a process change instead of a service;
- a service instead of a communication artifact;
- an interface change instead of communication;
- communication instead of a new interface;
- a simulator or practice environment instead of a course;
- a course or guided learning path instead of a simulator;
- stop or no-build.

For each material alternative, state:

- the class, not a fabricated full solution;
- the evidence or reasoning basis;
- the uncertainty and confidence boundary;
- what decision it could inform;
- that selection remains with the product owner.

Do not turn alternatives into an unsolicited strategy engagement or implement
one without owner authority.

## Minimum Product Validation

Minimum Product Validation strengthens the existing minimum hypothesis
validation. It is a conditional analytical recommendation inside Product Intent
Review, not a validation stage, research pipeline, product-research role,
required report, or permission to run a test.

Its governing question is:

> What is the cheapest, fastest, and most reversible way to obtain the evidence
> needed for the next decision?

It reduces one concrete uncertainty. It is not required to validate the whole
product or prove that the product will succeed.

### Decide Whether Validation Is Needed

Start from the one main product gap and record one analytical disposition:

- `minimum_test` — one bounded check can materially reduce the uncertainty for
  the next decision;
- `not_needed` — sufficient evidence already exists, the next move is cheap and
  reversible, the uncertainty does not affect it, the task is only local
  editing, the format is fixed outside scope, the requirement is mandatory, or
  the check would cost more than the decision;
- `insufficient` — one minimum check cannot responsibly support the decision
  because the error consequence, safety or rights exposure, irreversible
  investment, external dependency, conflicting evidence, audience
  heterogeneity, delayed effect, or unobservable outcome requires deeper
  evidence, specialist expertise, sequential checks, an owner decision, or a
  controlled rollout.

These values are analytical dispositions, not task statuses, lifecycle stages,
review outcomes, product findings, or owner decisions. In `not_needed`, state
why another check would not improve the next decision. In `insufficient`, state
why a bounded check cannot answer the material question and name the deeper
evidence or existing specialist route without creating a new pipeline.

### Select The Critical Hypothesis

When the disposition is `minimum_test`, select one primary critical hypothesis,
or a tightly related minimum set only when separating it would make the
observation non-diagnostic. A critical hypothesis is the claim on which the
next costly or hard-to-reverse step depends.

The critical hypothesis must:

- follow directly from the one main product gap;
- state one checkable claim rather than “test the product”;
- explain which next decision depends on the result;
- remain a hypothesis unless current evidence supports a stronger label;
- exclude secondary questions that cannot change that decision.

### Match Hypothesis And Method

Classify the primary uncertainty before choosing a method:

| Hypothesis class | What it tests | Suitable bounded methods | Method mismatch to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | Whether a material problem exists, for whom, in what context, with what consequences, and how it is handled now. | Interview, observation, existing-data or work-case analysis, incident review, workaround study. | Building or testing the proposed solution before the problem is supported. |
| Demand | Whether a person will voluntarily commit attention, time, effort, access, or another real resource. | Participation invitation, registration, alternative choice, application, access request, pre-enrollment, observable commitment. | Stated interest or liking treated as real demand. |
| Mechanism | Whether the key interaction can produce the required intermediate action, decision, or causal link. | Scenario test, short exercise, controlled walkthrough, test fragment, simulation, key-interaction prototype, pre/post decision comparison. | A satisfaction survey used to prove the mechanism. |
| Behavior | Whether real action changes, persists, or transfers into the work context. | Task observation, repeat task, delayed check, actual-usage analysis, work-decision comparison, limited field pilot. | Understanding, completion, or intention treated as behavior change. |
| Usability | Whether a person can understand and complete the key scenario and where interaction fails. | Prototype test, task-based usability test, observation, think-aloud, scenario walkthrough. | Demand research or a technical spike used to prove usability. |
| Feasibility | Whether data, technology, people, authority, process fit, risk, and cost permit implementation. | Technical spike, process walkthrough, limited integration, expert review, dependency audit, proof of concept. | User preference used to prove implementability. |
| Viability | Whether an organization can own, support, fund, and sustain the intervention without unacceptable process damage. | Owner and operating-model check, operating-cost review, process-impact review, bounded viability review. | A successful demo treated as sustainable operation, or commercial terminology imposed on an internal product. |

Method choice also depends on error consequence, next-step cost,
reversibility, evidence already available, audience access and context, and
ethical, privacy, safety, legal, and organizational constraints. Do not
automatically recommend an interview, survey, A/B test, statistical experiment,
prototype, or full-product pilot. A/B testing requires a suitable decision,
traffic, design, and evidence basis; a prototype is irrelevant when the
uncertainty is whether the problem exists.

### Minimum Validation Record

The smallest suitable existing analytical artifact records:

- critical hypothesis;
- why it is critical for the next decision;
- primary hypothesis class;
- bounded audience and context;
- selected method;
- minimum intervention or exposure;
- observable signal;
- continue condition;
- reconsider, reduce, change-class, research, or stop condition;
- inference limits: what the observation cannot prove;
- the next product-owner decision.

A proposed check is minimum only when it examines the critical hypothesis,
costs less than full implementation, excludes unrelated features and
production-quality finishing, can be stopped and reversed, and creates evidence
for the named next decision. Do not create a full course, service, campaign,
event, process, interface, or AI tool to test one premise.

Prefer observable action, choice, task completion, decision quality, repeat
use, skill transfer, error reduction, refusal, obstacle, or process change.
Stated liking, interest, clarity, approval, or intention may supplement
evidence but cannot alone confirm demand, mechanism, behavior change, transfer,
persistence, or operating effect.

Continue and reconsider conditions must be observable and decision-linked.
Qualitative conditions are valid when the evidence does not justify a numeric
threshold. A number is allowed only when its basis is recorded; never invent a
percentage, baseline, target, sample size, or significance claim for the
appearance of rigor.

Every check must state its inference limits. An interview may support the
existence of a problem but not demand for a course; a successful exercise may
support a mechanism but not long-term transfer; a prototype may support
usability but not technical feasibility; one-team use may not support scale;
registration may not support completion; positive feedback may not support
behavior change.

For a complex product, several checks may eventually be appropriate, but
Product Intent Review recommends only the closest check required for the next
decision. Do not produce a complete discovery or research program without
explicit authority.

For AI-related checks, account for automation bias, source-data quality, model
limits and variability, human control, confidentiality, permitted data use, and
the risk of judging AI by persuasive output rather than decision quality or
work effect. A successful demonstration is not proof of a stable operating
effect.

Minimum Product Validation must not substitute the AI Editorial Office for a
researcher, product owner, domain expert, ethics owner, measurement owner, or
other specialized authority; guarantee effectiveness; or authorize launch.

## Adaptive Output Contract

Product Intent Review has semantic output elements, not a universal rigid
template. Use only the elements required by the selected depth and decision:

- short product-intent verdict;
- reconstructed product logic;
- confirmed evidence;
- hypotheses and unknowns;
- one main product gap;
- bounded alternative classes;
- minimum product validation when needed, or a reason it is not needed or is
  insufficient;
- product finding;
- next product-owner decision;
- consequences for editorial production.

In `limited` mode, do not force every element or every seven-element-model row.
In `full` mode, preserve all material checks and decision boundaries, but still
use the smallest existing artifact that is reviewable and restartable.

A compact semantic shape may be:

```markdown

- confirmed:
- hypotheses and unknowns:
- main product gap:
- alternatives:
- minimum validation:
```

This shape is illustrative, not a required standalone artifact, task-object

### Reader-Facing Result

The result must help the task owner decide or validate what happens next. It
must not demonstrate internal analytical completeness. For `limited` and
`full`, use this semantic order inside the selected deliverable:

1. a direct product verdict;
2. one main product gap;
3. the next product-owner decision or check;
4. the evidence boundary and only the detail needed to understand the decision;
5. the editorial production consequence;
6. editorial or local material remarks after the product decision.

The first three elements must be understandable without reading the entire
artifact. Do not lead with language corrections, source/file inventories,
methodology, a long source recap, all model fields, all checks, or repeated
disclaimers.

In `limited`, keep the result to the assigned focus: one verdict, one gap,
confirmed versus hypothesis/unknown, the closest validation or explicit reason
it is not needed/insufficient, next decision, and production consequence. Do
not print the seven-element model, all four checks, a full validation plan,
long alternatives, a research narrative, or a product passport.

In `full`, include only decision-relevant reconstructed logic and strengths;
compact confirmed/hypothesis/unknown separation; one primary recommendation,
one strong alternative, and at most one reserve when alternatives matter;
practical minimum validation when required; owner decision; and production
consequence. Render validation compactly as what to test, how to test, what to
observe, how to decide, and what the check cannot prove. Seven model elements
may be combined or omitted when obvious and not decision-relevant. Full
analytical depth does not require a long result.

Use plain management language. Preserve `no-build`, stop, reroute, and
validate-first findings directly; do not replace them with vague potential,
development, or consideration language when the evidence supports a clearer
conclusion. State the evidence boundary once, then preserve calibrated wording
without repeating the same disclaimer after every paragraph.

Do not expose internal role names, pipeline semantics, task-local mode
implementation, canonical KB owners, lifecycle mechanics, or the internal
seven-checking process in a user-facing result unless the user explicitly asks
about the system. Source length must not mechanically determine result length.

Literal headings, tables, and a standalone Product Intent Review report are
optional. Use `/kb/deliverables/` to select the smallest existing form for the
reader job:

- `report` or a compact embedded block for a concise state/risk/next-decision
  result or a product dimension inside broader analysis;
- `decision-memo` when a known owner must choose among continue, reduce, pilot,
  change class, or stop and needs tradeoffs and an explicit ask;
- `research-report` when evidence quality, provenance, contradictions, or
  uncertainty are central to the decision;
- a separate task-local Product Intent Review report only when it is explicitly
  selected, full-depth traceability needs its own reader, or the result transfers
  independently and existing forms would distort the reader job.

Capability naming alone never justifies a deliverable profile or standalone
artifact.

## Product Finding To Editorial Consequence

The analytical owner forms the evidence-bounded product finding. Chief Editor
then translates it into one editorial production consequence:

- `Proceed` — intent is sufficient for the approved editorial work;
- `Proceed with constraints` — production is allowed only inside named scope,
  claim, format, uncertainty, or pilot boundaries;
- `Validate before production` — deep production waits while a prototype,
  pilot scenario, decision memo, research artifact, or other minimum validation
  is produced;
- `Reroute` — the selected deliverable or solution class no longer fits the
  finding and returns to Chief Editor;
- `Stop / no-build recommendation` — the editorial system recommends against
  creating the product in its current form while leaving the decision to the
  product owner.

These labels describe editorial permission and constraint only. They are not
task statuses, review outcomes, lifecycle stages, pipelines, or automatic
product decisions.

For `limited` and `full`, a sufficient finding precedes deep editorial
production. Structure extraction, compact summary, evidence mapping, logic
reconstruction, and a minimum validation artifact may precede the finding.
Full restructuring, long-form or series production, full-course design, and UX
copy for unapproved behavior may not.

When production exposes a new material product gap, changed audience/goal/
format, unsupported mechanism, incompatible deliverable, negative or ambiguous
validation result, or need to change product behavior, the production role
returns to Chief Editor. Reroute is not a task status, and Writer Agent or UX
Writer must not redesign the product independently.

The compact Editorial Decision Frame carries only mode/focus, product finding,
one main gap, evidence boundary, production consequence and permission, owner
decision required, and reconsideration trigger. Detailed model/check evidence,
alternatives, and validation design remain in the selected analytical artifact.

## Product Finding Versus Operational Verdict

A product finding is the semantic conclusion of the lens. It may say, for
example, that:

- the intent is coherent enough for bounded editorial production;
- the intent is usable only under named constraints;
- a material hypothesis needs a minimum test before deep production;
- the proposed intervention class is weak relative to an alternative class;
- the available evidence does not support a product-owner decision;
- no-build or stop should be considered by the product owner.

An operational verdict is the existing review outcome:

- `approved`;
- `changes_requested`;
- `blocked`.

These are not interchangeable. A product finding:

- is not a task status;
- is not a review outcome;
- is not a pipeline or lifecycle state;
- does not automatically approve, block, launch, test, or stop anything;
- does not replace Review Agent judgment;
- does not replace the product owner's decision authority.

| Professional Analysis | Analytical product shape, synthesis, options, implications, recommendation, uncertainty, next action. | Owns the seven-element product-intent model and four product checks. | Product Intent Review is a narrow child lens; Professional Analysis remains the general family owner. |
| Analytical Reasoning | Framing, decomposition, hypotheses, contradiction, disconfirmation, sufficiency. | Applies those moves to one bounded product-intent decision. | Analytical Reasoning owns cognitive method; Product Intent Review owns the domain-specific semantic contract. |
| Editorial Evidence Framework | Evidence classes, confidence, assumptions, unknowns, validation discipline. | States which product-intent claims need calibration. | Product Intent Review never creates a parallel taxonomy; Evidence Framework remains evidence owner. |
| Audience and Outcome Alignment | Reader/user context, intended outcome, starting state, required change, usefulness fit. | Distinguishes the intervention's value/mechanism from the artifact's audience fit. | Audience and Outcome Alignment owns artifact/readership alignment; Product Intent Review owns intervention-intent coherence. |
| Reader-centered quality | Clarity, usefulness, cognitive bridge, application path, reader burden. | Questions whether the product/intervention deserves the reader-facing artifact. | Reader quality cannot prove product value; the relevant editorial owner retains quality decisions. |
| Deliverable Knowledge | Deliverable purpose, fit, limitations, failure modes, and companion relations. | Separates deliverable choice from product or intervention choice. | Deliverable Knowledge owns artifact-type fit; Product Intent Review owns the underlying intervention intent. |
| Editorial Planning | Option generation, evaluation, tradeoffs, selected approach, reconsideration triggers. | Prioritizes the main product gap and bounded intervention-class alternatives. | Planning owns plan selection; Product Intent Review supplies product-intent constraints and leaves the choice to the owner. |
| Architecture Review | Architecture drivers, quality scenarios, design fitness, tradeoffs, accepted architecture risk. | Applies to product/intervention intent beyond architecture. | Architecture Review owns architecture decisions; route architecture questions there rather than generalizing Product Intent Review. |
| Editorial Challenge Lens | Challenges assumptions, route validity, evidence, and unwanted consequences in the existing review context. | Provides the positive pre-production product-intent model and checks. | Challenge remains a review capability; Product Intent Review does not create a second review gate. |
| Review Agent | Independent challenge and operational verdict. | Produces a semantic product finding for the reviewed task evidence. | Review Agent decides `approved`, `changes_requested`, or `blocked`; Product Intent Review never does. |
| Product owner | Owns product direction, scope, investment, pilot, launch, change-class, and stop decisions. | Supplies evidence-bounded analysis and alternatives. | Product Intent Review advises; the product owner decides and remains accountable. |

Specialized legal, ethical, safety, accessibility, research, architecture,
engineering, finance, operational, and domain decisions remain with their
existing or human owners. Product Intent Review may identify the dependency but
must not simulate the missing authority.

## Cooperation Through Existing Roles

This section describes capability cooperation only. It does not amend role
files or implement routing.

| Existing role | Product Intent Review cooperation |

## Canonical Usage Examples

These examples illustrate boundaries only. They are not a replacement for the
evaluation catalogue or a new routing table.

### A — `not_needed`

Request: edit wording for an approved product and fixed interaction.

- Negative evidence: product logic, audience, behavior, and solution class are
  explicitly outside scope.
- Result: ordinary compact editing; no Product Intent block or analytical owner
  is loaded.

### B — `limited`

Request: prepare materials for an approved activity when one behavior-change
mechanism is still uncertain.

- Focus: only whether the material mechanism can support the intended action.
- Result: one evidence-bounded finding and one bounded next owner decision;
  unrelated product elements remain out of scope.

### C — `full`

Request: assess and describe a new intervention while the owner is still
deciding whether it should exist.

- Result: product-first reconstruction, four material checks, one main gap,
  bounded alternatives, the nearest valid validation or an honest
  `insufficient` disposition, and an editorial production consequence.

### D — approved no-build

Finding: available evidence does not support the proposed intervention class,
and no-build or reroute is recommended.

- Review result: `approved` when the analysis, evidence boundaries, authority,
  and communication are sound.
- Product decision: remains with the product owner; the operational verdict
  does not silently make that decision.

## Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Warning sign | Required correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overactivation | Every substantial editorial task receives a product review. | Recheck materiality and use `not_needed` or `limited`. |
| Keyword activation | “Product,” “course,” “UX,” or similar wording triggers the lens alone. | Require multi-signal evidence and a consequence of skipping the lens. |
| Hidden universal brief | The seven elements become mandatory fields for every task. | Restore the adaptive semantic contract and smallest existing artifact rule. |
| Consulting drift | The lens expands into strategy, market, service-design, or research work not requested. | Return to the bounded next decision and name external expertise needed. |
| Product-owner substitution | The analysis chooses launch, scope, intervention, or investment. | Present evidence and alternatives; return the decision to the owner. |
| False certainty | Needs, demand, motivation, causality, or effect are stated as facts without evidence. | Reclassify as hypothesis or unknown through the Evidence Framework. |
| Analysis after polish | Product weakness is examined only after substantial production. | Route the material question before deep production when authorized. |
| Deliverable/product conflation | A report, course, page, campaign, or interface is treated as proof of product value. | Separate artifact purpose from intervention value and mechanism. |
| Weak validation | Clicks, attendance, completion, one quote, or one case are treated as proof. | State inference limits and define a decision-relevant minimum signal. |
| Ritual validation | A test is added when evidence is sufficient or the uncertainty cannot affect the next move. | State why validation is `not_needed` and proceed within the evidence boundary. |
| Validation theater | A full-product pilot, survey, A/B test, or invented threshold creates the appearance of rigor without testing the critical uncertainty. | Select the critical hypothesis, fit the method, remove unrelated scope, and record the threshold basis or use an observable qualitative condition. |
| Wrong method | Attitude is used to prove behavior, a prototype to prove problem existence, or a demo to prove viability. | Match the primary hypothesis class to an observable method and signal. |
| Research-program overreach | The result designs every future check instead of the evidence needed now. | Recommend only the closest check for the next owner decision. |
| Hidden insufficiency | A bounded check is proposed even though safety, irreversibility, heterogeneity, dependency, duration, or observability makes it non-diagnostic. | Record `insufficient` and route the deeper evidence or specialist need without creating a pipeline. |
| Specification bloat | The capability copies evidence, planning, audience, review, or architecture contracts. | Keep only product-intent semantics and point to canonical owners. |
| Role drift | Writer, Research, Review, or AI assumes product, domain, or research authority. | Restore existing role and human-owner boundaries. |
| Finding/verdict conflation | A product finding is labeled `approved`, `changes_requested`, or `blocked`. | Separate semantic finding from Review Agent operational verdict. |
| Creating an alternative instead of checking | The AI designs a replacement product in detail. | Name only bounded alternative classes and their basis. |
| Refusal without evidence | Uncertainty automatically becomes a stop or blocked recommendation. | State what can still be analyzed, what cannot be decided, and what minimum evidence is needed. |

Verification assets:

- case catalogue: `tests/fixtures/product_intent_evaluation/cases.json`;

- runner self-check and coverage gates:
  `tests/test_product_intent_evaluation.sh`;

Run from the repository root:

```bash
sh ai-editorial-office/tests/test_product_intent_evaluation.sh
```

The runner checks deterministic metadata, coverage, routing, structured
contract behavior, critical failures, and recorded manual-judgment completeness.
Independent expert judgment remains responsible for main-gap quality, product
finding proportionality, consequence fit, authority, validation quality, and
communication; automation must not replace it with string matching or one
aggregate score.

Add or change a case only for a new material task class, a reproducible failure
mode, a canonical-contract change, or required regression protection. Define
expected results as semantic properties, acceptable finding ranges, allowed
variability, and forbidden critical errors rather than one preferred wording.

A production change requires a failing case and a documented defect. Patch the
canonical owner minimally, rerun neighboring and full suites, record every
production repair loop, and obtain independent review. Do not change expected
behavior merely to make current output pass. Protect against overfit with
contrast pairs, negative cases, multiple classes, adversarial cases, acceptable
answer variability, and regression coverage outside the changed fixture.

## Completion Criteria

Product Intent Review is sufficient for its selected task-local depth when:

- activation or non-activation has an evidence-based reason;
- confirmed evidence, hypotheses, and unknowns are not conflated;
- the relevant product-intent elements and checks are visible;
- one main gap is prioritized when a material gap exists;
- alternatives remain bounded classes and owner choice is preserved;
- validation follows from the one main gap and either identifies the critical
  hypothesis or honestly records `not_needed` / `insufficient`;
- the selected method fits the hypothesis, risk, context, and evidence state;
- a proposed check is smaller than full implementation, stoppable, reversible,
  and limited to the nearest decision;
- the observable signal, continue/reconsider conditions, threshold basis when
  numeric, inference limits, and next owner decision are explicit;
- weak attitudinal evidence is not presented as proof of demand, mechanism,
  behavior, transfer, persistence, or operating effect;
- the product finding is distinct from the operational review verdict;
- consequences for editorial production and the next owner decision are clear;
- adjacent canonical owners are referenced rather than duplicated;
- the result remains inside the existing lifecycle and role architecture.
