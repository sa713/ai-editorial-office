# Interview

This is a deliverable knowledge profile, not a template or pipeline.

## Purpose

Publish a person's perspective, expertise, experience, or argument through an
attributed question-and-answer or edited conversational form.

## Best Use Cases

- the speaker's voice and authority are part of the value;
- questions can reveal experience, reasoning, or disagreement;
- attribution and editorial context are acceptable.

## Weak Use Cases

- the main value is neutral synthesis across sources;
- claims cannot be verified or attribution is unsafe;
- a structured procedure or decision record is required.

## Typical Reader Goal

Learn how a named person thinks, experienced the issue, or explains the topic.

## Typical Structure

Editorial context, purposeful question sequence, attributed answers,
clarifications or fact notes, and a closing implication or next step.

## Strengths

- preserves voice and lived context;
- creates natural movement through questions;
- makes uncertainty and perspective visible.

## Weaknesses

- dependent on source quality;
- conversational order may be inefficient;
- attribution and editing require care.

## Common Failure Modes

- generic questions;
- transcript dump without editorial shaping;
- speaker claims presented as verified fact;
- over-editing that changes the speaker's meaning.

## Typical Companion Deliverables

- [Announcement](announcement.md) for publication distribution;
- [Article](article.md) for editorial synthesis when separately justified;
- [FAQ](faq.md) for follow-up audience questions.

## Not This

An interview is not an [Article](article.md): the source voice and question
structure remain central rather than disappearing into an authorial narrative.
