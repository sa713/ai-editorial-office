# Comparison Matrix

This is a deliverable knowledge profile, not a template or pipeline.

## Purpose

Compare options consistently against shared criteria, evidence, tradeoffs, and
decision context.

## Best Use Cases

- several options must be evaluated for a purchase, policy, design, or route;
- criteria can be named and applied comparably;
- the reader needs differences visible at a glance.

## Weak Use Cases

- criteria are unknown, incomparable, or highly narrative;
- one option needs deep explanation before comparison;
- a final approval ask is the primary job.

## Typical Reader Goal

See which option fits which need and understand the evidence behind tradeoffs.

## Typical Structure

Decision context, criteria definitions, option-by-criterion comparison,
evidence notes, limitations, and bounded conclusion when requested.

## Strengths

- makes comparison explicit and scannable;
- exposes missing evidence;
- reduces inconsistent option treatment.

## Weaknesses

- compresses nuance;
- invites arbitrary criteria or pseudo-precision;
- wide matrices are hard to read.

## Common Failure Modes

- decorative table without decision criteria;
- hidden weighting;
- unlike evidence compared as equal;
- recommendation that outruns the matrix evidence.

## Typical Companion Deliverables

- [Decision Memo](decision-memo.md) for the recommendation and ask;
- [Report](report.md) for supporting analysis.

## Not This

A comparison matrix is not a [Decision Memo](decision-memo.md): it structures
evidence across options but does not automatically own the final recommendation.
